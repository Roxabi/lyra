## Problem

`roxabi-contracts` currently exposes two top-level sibling subject families for
job outcomes — `factory.results.<job_id>` and `factory.progress.<job_id>` —
that were defined alongside #1045 before the unified `factory.job.<id>.*`
taxonomy was ratified in §15.6 of the concept analysis. The NATS subject space
now has two parallel hierarchies for the same concept: the old siblings live as
`Literal` constants in `packages/roxabi-contracts/src/roxabi_contracts/jobs/subjects.py`
(`result_prefix`, `progress_prefix`) and in the `jobs_result()` / `jobs_progress()`
helper return values, while `docs/architecture/job-model.md` already treats
`factory.job.<id>.result` and `factory.job.<id>.progress` as the SSoT target.

The window to fix this cheaply is now: confirmed 0 callers of either helper in
`src/factory`, 0 satellite consumers across llmCLI/voiceCLI/imageCLI, and 0
references in the ACL matrix. Once #1812 (OmpWorker) lands on top, the new
`factory.job.<id>.*` subjects gain real in-repo callers and the rename becomes a
multi-repo coordinated migration instead of a pure contracts refactor.

The `JobProgress` model docstring (line 72 of `models.py`) still references the
old `factory.progress.<job_id>` string. Satellites deriving the publish subject
from the model docstring or from the constants would misroute silently at
runtime — not a type error, not a test failure, just a silent wrong subject.

## Premise Validity

**Success in 6 months:** `packages/roxabi-contracts` exports only the
`factory.job.<id>.*` subject family for per-job facets; `factory.results` and
`factory.progress` subject strings are absent from all of `src/factory`,
satellite repos, and the NATS wire subject space; #1812 OmpWorker uses
`jobs_result()` / `jobs_progress()` / `jobs_steer()` / `jobs_opened()` /
`jobs_closed()` exclusively; the deprecated aliases have been removed in a
follow-up tagged release.

**Failure in 6 months:** A new in-repo caller introduces `factory.results.<x>`
or `factory.progress.<x>` strings directly (bypassing helpers) because the
migration window closed without a grep gate; OR #1812 is forced to use old
subject strings because this issue stalls, creating a split taxonomy in
production.

**Simplest alternative:** Leave the sibling prefixes as-is and only add the
new `factory.job.<id>.*` helpers alongside them, treating both as valid until
the old ones have real callers to migrate.

**Why not simplest:** The dual taxonomy would immediately be codified by #1812
(which uses new subjects) while the old constants remain in `__all__` — any
future reader or satellite would face two equally-visible but contradictory
families. The cheap-now window (0 callers, 0 ACL refs) expires once #1812
lands. Retiring the siblings while cost is zero is the explicit rationale
stated in the issue body.
