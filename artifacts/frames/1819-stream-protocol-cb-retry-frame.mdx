## Problem

The `LlmProvider` streaming path is less resilient than the non-streaming path, and the
**recheck premise correction** narrows (but does not eliminate) the gap the issue describes:

- `complete()` is protected by the full stack: `CircuitBreakerDecorator → RetryDecorator → driver`.
- `stream()` **is already declared in the Protocol** (`core/ports/llm.py:67-75`) — contrary to the
  issue body ("hors du Protocol"). It is declared *optional*: callers gate on `hasattr(provider, "stream")`,
  and `llm/CLAUDE.md` says "do not add it to the Protocol until all drivers implement it" (a doc
  contradiction, since the signature is already present).
- The two decorators **do** define `stream()` (`decorators.py:83-96, 147-160`) — but both deliberately
  pass through with **no protection**:
  - `RetryDecorator.stream()` → `"no retry — stream is live data"`
  - `CircuitBreakerDecorator.stream()` → `"circuit check applies to complete() only"`
- The NATS path (`LlmClient`) already gets circuit-breaking at the **pool layer**
  (`WorkerPoolClient`, #1278). The fully-unprotected case is the **in-process `ClaudeCliDriver`**
  streaming path — no pool layer, no CB, no retry.

**Why now:** issue #1814 flips the slice from HITL → auto. Streaming becomes the majority
user-visible path at higher volume. A wedged/unhealthy `ClaudeCliDriver` during streaming would
hang every streaming turn with no breaker to trip — the cascade the issue flags. The fix is the
gating dependency to land **before** the #1814 flip.

## Who

- **Primary:** end users on streaming turns (Telegram/Discord) when the in-process CLI driver is
  unhealthy — today they get hangs/cascades instead of a clean fail-fast.
- **Secondary:** operators (no circuit-open signal in logs for streaming → harder to diagnose);
  the #1814 auto-flip work, which is blocked on this resilience parity.

## Constraints

- **Idempotency:** an async iterator cannot be naively replayed — retry is only safe **before the
  first event is yielded** (connect/first-token failure). Mid-stream failures must not retry.
- **Layering:** `llm/` must not import `adapters/` or `infrastructure/`; decorators stay pure.
- **File length:** `decorators.py` is 164 SLOC; ≤300 SLOC gate applies — keep additions tight.
- **Doc reconciliation:** `llm/CLAUDE.md` and the `core/ports/llm.py` comment must be brought into
  agreement on stream()'s Protocol status.
- **No double-CB on NATS path:** `LlmClient` already carries pool-layer CB; wiring must not stack a
  second breaker on it (per `llm/CLAUDE.md`: decorator-CB reserved for `ClaudeCliDriver`).

## Out of Scope

- NATS pool-layer circuit-breaking (already delivered in #1278).
- Making `stream()` a **required** Protocol member across all drivers (drivers that don't stream
  stay hasattr-gated) — unless trivial.
- The #1814 HITL→auto flip itself.
- Per-turn wall-clock deadline (caller responsibility per `llm/CLAUDE.md`).

## Premise Validity

**Success in 6 months:** Streaming turns have resilience parity with `complete()` for the
in-process driver: when `ClaudeCliDriver` is unhealthy the `CircuitBreakerDecorator.stream()`
short-circuits (open circuit → error event, no hang) and records success/failure to drive the
breaker; connect-time stream failures retry; mid-stream failures do not. Verified by unit tests
covering open-circuit, record-failure-on-stream-error, and no-retry-after-first-event.

**Failure in 6 months:** After the #1814 auto-flip, a `ClaudeCliDriver` streaming outage still
cascades — observable as a streaming error/timeout spike with **zero** circuit-open short-circuits
in logs, i.e. `CircuitBreakerDecorator.stream()` still passes through and CB-on-stream test
coverage is 0. (Falsifiable: breaker never trips on the streaming path under sustained driver
failure.)

**Simplest alternative:** Leave `stream()` unprotected and rely on the NATS pool-layer CB plus the
transport's 300s per-chunk timeout.
**Why not simplest:** the in-process `ClaudeCliDriver` path has **no** pool layer and **no** CB —
under the #1814 auto volume a wedged CLI subprocess hangs every streaming turn with nothing to
trip, which is exactly the cascade #1819 exists to prevent. The 300s per-chunk timeout bounds
silence between chunks, not a never-started stream.
